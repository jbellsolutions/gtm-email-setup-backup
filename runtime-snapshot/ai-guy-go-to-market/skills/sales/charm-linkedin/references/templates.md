# LinkedIn Template Library (Charm Offensive)

Adapt, don't blindly copy. Keep the voice from `voice-and-humour.md`. LinkedIn invites have a tight
character limit — keep them short.

---

## Invite copy — just for getting connections (works at scale)

> Hey hey {firstname},
> I really wanted to connect. As such, I've decided not to send you the generic LinkedIn invite!
> Fingers crossed,
> {Name}

Notes: even a generic invite gets a lot of accepts; something a little more human works well at
scale. Personalised invites are best for key prospects — always do those by hand.

---

## Invite copy — the "One/Two Punch" lead-gen template

> Hey hey {firstname},
> Nice to 'meet' you. I have a weird skill that means I can {your offering — e.g. book meetings with
> just about anybody}.
> If you're at all curious, click 'accept'. :)
> If not, send me your most cutting insult. :(
> Fingers crossed,
> {Name}

Notes: the "cutting insult" line adds humour to the CTA, gives them an easy way out, and sets up the
follow-up message. Replace the weird-skill line with your real offering.

---

## Message after they accept (the "spill the beans")

> Hello there {firstname},
> Thanks for not sending me a cutting insult!
> If you're at all curious about the ideas I mentioned, let me know and I'll spill the beans.
> I say 'ideas'… it's of course a thinly veiled sales pitch. However, I often get too enthusiastic
> and give away great ideas. You should take advantage of this!
> Have an absolutely wonderful day,
> {Name}

Notes: immediately references the humour in the invite (callback); disarming honesty ("thinly veiled
sales pitch"); frames it as *them* gaining, not being hard-sold.

---

## Sector-specific follow-up (after connecting, when you didn't use the one/two punch)

> Thanks for connecting with me, {firstname}. I hope you don't regret this as I send you this very
> brief sales pitch!
> - My name is {name} and I'm a {what you do}
> - My passion is {sector}… and I'd love to work with more clients in this sector
> - This message is my attempt at getting your attention!
> I'd love to give you some ideas you're free to steal.
> Let me know if you'd like to chat.
> Have a great day,
> {Name}

Notes: make light of the sudden pitch; show you love the sector they're in; make it look like they
stand to gain rather than being hard-sold.

---

## Sequencing tips

- Invite → (accept) → follow-up within a day or two while the invite is fresh.
- If no reply to the follow-up, one light nudge later — never a pressure chase.
- Keep every message shorter than a cold email: one hook, one soft ask.
- For key prospects, replace the templated middle with a genuine, researched, specific detail.
