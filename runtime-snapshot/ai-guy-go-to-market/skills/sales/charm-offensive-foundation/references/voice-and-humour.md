# The Charm Offensive Voice & Humour Toolkit

This is the canonical voice reference for every `charm-*` skill. Read it before drafting.

## Why this works — the Humour Interrupt

Prospects are drowning in sales messages, and technology makes it trivial for your competitors to
reach them too. You have to win attention and *keep* winning it through the whole message. Humour
does this while making the prospect smile or laugh — you connect human-to-human and give them a good
feeling remotely, which makes the sales pitch underneath far more palatable. Charm disarms.

## Core voice principles

1. **Disarming honesty.** Say the quiet part out loud. Where you got their details, that this is a
   pitch, that you're nervous. Honesty about an awkward thing is unexpected, and unexpected + honest
   + funny is a killer combination.
2. **Ideas they're free to steal.** Frame the value as a gift. "I'd love to give you some ideas
   you're free to steal." Make it look like *they* gain, not like they're being hard-sold.
3. **Low-pressure, escape-hatch CTAs.** Always give an easy out, ideally a funny one. "Hit reply, or
   send me your most cutting insult." "Even if it's just to tell me to politely go away."
4. **Be a human, not a brand.** Warmth, personality, self-deprecation. No jargon, no buzzwords, no
   corporate throat-clearing.
5. **Brevity + scannability.** Short lines. White space. Never a wall of text. Make them *want* to
   scroll.
6. **Genuine only.** Never fake a compliment, a shared interest, a stat, or a testimonial. If it
   isn't true, don't write it.

## Signature moves (the recognisable Charm props)

- **The list-worthy opener (the Reverse):** "You've never heard of me. Hi, I'm {name}. I got your
  details from a list. *gasp!* But hey — at least you're list-worthy, that's gotta be worth
  something, right?"
- **The favourite weapon:** attach a photo of a pet/animal doing something absurd (the ferret in
  bunny ears named "Colin"). "I've attached a picture of a ferret that's been dressed up. According
  to the internet, his name is Colin. I trust this will charm you into submission."
- **The self-deprecating follow-up photo:** a sad-face photo when they didn't reply.
- **Three job titles in the signature:** serious / descriptive / silly. e.g. "Director / Veteran of
  B2B Sales / Writer of Terrible Fiction" — or "Free Trial Conversion Specialist / SaaS Lover /
  Below Average Runner."
- **Warm, slightly old-fashioned sign-offs:** "Have an absolutely wonderful day," / "Stay vigilant," /
  "Fingers crossed!" / "I await your profanity-filled response."
- **The 5-bullet rule:** when you list reasons you're writing, use five — "I can't end this list on
  number 4. Five bullets just looks and feels better."

## The ten humour formulas

Use these sparingly and deliberately. One or two per message. (From "Disarm with Charm".)

1. **The Reverse** — build a premise, then immediately misdirect.
   "I got your contact details from a list. *gasp!* but hey, at least you're list-worthy, that's
   gotta be worth something right?!"

2. **Triples (the power of 3)** — first two elements set a pattern, the third breaks it.
   "If you agree to meet, I'll take you for **coffee / lunch / tequila shots**…"
   Also great for job titles: serious, descriptive, silly.

3. **Lists** — add one absurd item to an otherwise normal list, then optionally call it out.
   "We offer copywriting, social media consultancy, video production, analytics audits and
   **bedroom tidying**." → "One of these isn't true. I'll leave you to decide which."

4. **Triple Reverse** — build with the power of 3, then undercut it.
   "I'd like to introduce myself in a way that shows I'm **interesting, witty and clever**. Alas, I
   wrote this email instead."

5. **Understatement** — moderate/tentative phrasing so claims don't sound arrogant.
   "The client **wasn't displeased** with the results." / "It'll be the best thing you've done **all
   hour**." / "I was in a **spot of bother**."

6. **Exaggeration** — over-the-top for a pain point, then an honest reassessment.
   "Contracts so restrictive they come with a set of **complimentary handcuffs**."
   "We win clients from those **evil** (ok, not evil… but not as **good**) other agencies."

7. **Incongruent Absurdity** — something absurd in a business context, delivered in formal language.
   "I've attached a picture of a ferret dressed up. According to the internet, his name is Colin. I
   trust this will **charm you into submission**." / "I await your **profanity-filled** response."
   Formula: [Formal & Assumptive] + [Surprise].

8. **Funny Words** — a single unusual word as a pattern interrupt, surrounded by serious ones.
   "I await your **baffled** response with great interest." Word bank: bamboozled, hoodwinked,
   flummoxed, tomfoolery, horseplay, antics, shenanigans, frolics, besmirched. Use sparingly.

9. **Oxymorons** — "borderline fascinating", "unbiased opinion", "open secret", "deafening silence",
   "I'm 99% certain of it".

10. **Parody / Memes** — invent a fake product/app that mocks the prospect's pain (e.g. "Agency
    Bullshit Guard", "Cold Call Bingo", "Auto-Brand-Police"). Memes pack a whole premise into one
    image so the overlaid line delivers the punch.

## A worked example (the "magic" cold email skeleton)

> Bonjour {{first name}},
> You've never heard of me. Hi, I'm {name}. I got your email from {honest/funny source}.
> Let me get right down to it…
> I don't know if this is a problem you have, but I help {who} {solve what}.
> {Risk reversal / no-brainer line, if you have one.}
> If it's not a problem, that's totally cool. If it is — would you be open to a quick 7-minute chat?
> I promise to give you some ideas you're free to steal, and I might even tell you my favourite joke.
> Finally, it's time to release my favourite weapon: {pet photo}.
> I await your stern but fair reply,
> {Name}
> {Serious title} / {Descriptive title} / {Funny title}

## Self-check before sending (run this every time)

- Did I open with a genuine pattern interrupt, not "Hope this email finds you well"?
- Is there at least one honest admission and one moment of humour — but not too many?
- Is the value framed as a gift ("ideas you're free to steal"), not a hard sell?
- Is the CTA low-pressure with an easy/funny out?
- Short lines, scannable, no jargon, no wall of text?
- Is every fact, compliment, and claim genuinely true?
- Does it sound like a specific human (the user), not a brand?
- Readability: aim for ~grade 6 (short words, short sentences). Cut adverbs and passive voice.
