# Candidate Interview Review

Use this skill when the user provides interview transcripts, recordings, screenshots, or notes and needs a hiring assessment, candidate comparison workbook, recruiter/client update, or follow-up email.

## Core workflow

1. **Collect sources first.** Read every transcript and screenshot supplied. Identify candidates, dates, role context, interviewers, and explicit sharing constraints.
2. **Normalize interviews.** Build a common comparison schema from repeated or adjacent questions. Useful columns include background, strengths, weaknesses/development areas, CRM/GHL experience, tools, customer/outreach experience, organization/data handling, communication/phone skills, availability/commitment, and objective assessment.
3. **Separate evidence from judgment.** Distinguish what the candidate stated from the reviewer’s assessment. Use “stated,” “reported,” “demonstrated,” or “no hands-on experience was evident.” Do not infer beyond the transcript.
4. **Keep feedback professional and shareable.** Exclude personal comments, jokes, sensitive details, or title-log remarks that could be misread or forwarded. If aggregate feedback is requested, do not include candidate-specific criticism in the email.
5. **Assess role-critical gaps explicitly.** Compare candidates against the actual role requirements. For GoHighLevel, distinguish no exposure, awareness only, exploratory/free-tier use, and production-level hands-on experience.
6. **Handle recording caveats carefully.** Mention poor audio only as an evaluation limitation, not as candidate criticism. Do not claim it affected a specific candidate unless supported.
7. **Produce the artifact.** For a workbook, put candidates in rows and normalized questions/categories in columns; include a separate assessment sheet. For an email, keep the body concise and use the workbook for details.
8. **Offer concrete recruiting next steps.** If no candidate is a clear fit, say so directly and optionally propose publishing a job post, sourcing through private communities/job boards, targeting specified universities, and coordinating more interviews.
9. **Verify delivery.** Re-open the workbook/document. Check sheet names, headers, candidate count, attachment names, recipients, and tone. Never claim an attachment exists without verifying its saved path.

## Email standards

- Confirm To, CC, sender, greeting, attachment filename, and Slack/channel follow-up.
- Use a straightforward, businesslike tone with modest warmth.
- Lead with the aggregate finding: candidates may be generally capable while lacking role-critical technical experience.
- Avoid naming or ranking candidates in the email unless explicitly requested; put detailed comparisons in the workbook.
- Make recruiting proposals optional and concrete: “If you would like, I can…”
- Do not include private remarks merely because they appeared in an interview log or screenshot.

## Spreadsheet standards

- Use wrapped text, frozen panes, filters, readable widths, and a separate assessment tab.
- Condense repetitive transcript material into concise evidence-based notes.
- Include a source-files or methodology note.
- Do not fabricate scores or rankings; use qualitative assessment when no scoring rubric exists.
- Recalculate and validate formulas if any are used; transcript summaries normally need none.

## Pitfalls

- “Heard of GHL,” “used a free trial,” and “worked in GHL production” are not equivalent.
- A self-described weakness is not a definitive diagnosis.
- Do not overstate AI experience when the candidate only described a limited assistant or coworking feature.
- Do not place sensitive or embarrassing details in a client-facing email when an objective aggregate summary will do.
- Do not silently omit a requested attachment or channel-access follow-up.
