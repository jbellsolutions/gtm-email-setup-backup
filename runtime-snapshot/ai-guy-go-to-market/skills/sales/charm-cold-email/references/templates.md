# Cold Email Template Library (Charm Offensive)

Adapt these — don't send verbatim. They are the proven skeletons. Swap `{…}` and `{{merge}}` fields
for real, researched detail. Keep the voice from `voice-and-humour.md`.

## The "Magic" cold email (book a sales call)

Best single-shot email for booking a short call. Annotated real example (Aaron Krall):

> **Subject:** {intriguing, curiosity-driven — e.g. "the drunk cold email that made me £1m"}
>
> Bonjour {{first name}},
> You've never heard of me. Hi, I'm {name}. I got your email from {top-secret-servers joke}… ok, I
> got it from {honest source}. *(The Reverse: build a silly premise, then honest admission.)*
> Let me get right down to it…
> I don't know if this is a problem you have, but I help {audience} {solve specific problem}.
> {Risk reversal — e.g. "I'll do X, you do Y, and if you don't see results you don't owe a penny.
> No high fees, contracts or blood oaths."}
> I'm not sure if this is a problem you have, and if it's not, that's totally cool.
> If it is — would you be open to a quick 7-minute chat? I promise to be somewhat useful, and I
> might even tell you my favourite joke.
> Finally, it's time to release my favourite weapon: {a photo of your pet doing something absurd}.
> I await your stern but fair reply,
> {Name}
> {Serious title} / {Descriptive title} / {Funny title}

Why it works: intriguing subject → honest/funny opener (pattern interrupt) → gets to the point fast →
gives an out → tiny, low-pressure ask → "ideas you're free to steal" → absurd image → human signature.

## The 8-part cold email sequence

Send steps a few days apart. Escalate charm and self-deprecation, never pressure. Personalise at
least the first name; more if you can.

**Email #1 — The intro (the magic email above).** Honest opener, "I help…", 7-minute ask, pet photo,
three job titles. End: "I await your profanity-filled response. Have an absolutely terrific day,".

**Email #2 — The P.S. bump (a few hours/1 day later).**
> Sorry, forgot to mention — you can find more info at {www.url.com}. I'll get back to nervously
> anticipating your response.
> {First name}
> — Sent from my iPhone
*(No unsubscribe link on this short personal-style nudge; keep it human.)*

**Email #3 — The sad photo.**
> Hey {{first}},
> I'll be honest — I emailed last week and didn't get a reply, and I'm… well, I'll let my photo do
> the talking. [insert self-deprecating sad-face photo]
> If you could hit 'reply', even just to tell me to politely 'go away', it'd put a smile on my face.
> Fingers crossed!

**Email #4 — The value/stat angle (more "professional").**
> Howdy {{first_name}}, it's me, {name}, again. Trying a more sensible approach this time. Wish me luck!
> I found an article that shows {a genuinely interesting, relevant stat}. {link}
> I thought this might interest you because {real reason}.
> Fingers crossed you accidentally hit 'reply' instead of 'delete'. Stay vigilant,

**Email #5 — The light touch.**
> Hi {{first}}, I really hope I'm not causing you to do this… [mildly funny image]
> A quick reply is enough — whether you'd like to talk about {offering} or not.
> I hope you have a wonderful hour, day and week, {{first}}!

**Email #6 — Three convenient CTAs.**
> {{first_name}}, I know you're busy, so I've prepared 3 convenient calls to action:
> 1. Ignore this and eventually I'll get the picture and write terrible poetry about the deal we
>    never did. {MOST POPULAR}
> 2. Hit 'reply' and I'll do the same. {RECOMMENDED}
> 3. WILDCARD — call me on {number}. Interrupt my day like I have yours. I deserve it! {LIMITED TIME}
> Option 2 is my favourite!

**Email #7 — The pure smile.** A short line + an amusing picture. "Hope this brightens up your day,".

**Email #8 — The polite breakup.**
> Hi {{first_name}}, I've reached out a couple of times but didn't hear back — I may have jumped the
> gun assuming you were ready to {review your offering}. My intent isn't to be a pain, so this'll be
> my last follow-up. But if I'm mistaken and you're open to connecting, send me a quick email.
> Either way, I wish you all the best. Cheers,

## Direct Mail → Follow-up Email combo ("my favourite weapon")

Two memorable touches beat one. Open rates on the follow-up email can hit 70%+.

1. **Send a letter first.** Typewriter/monospace font, **no company logo** (so it stands out), on a
   bright or handwritten-address envelope, ideally with a **wax seal**. Handwritten letters and even
   postcards work superbly — a postcard doesn't even need opening.
2. Use the same charm skeleton as the magic email, adapted to a letter: list-worthy opener, honest
   "let me get down to brass tacks", what you do, "I'd love to buy you coffee / lunch / tequila
   shots", the 3 convenient CTAs, an absurd enclosure (the ferret sticker "Colin"), warm sign-off.
3. **A few days later, send an email** that references the letter **in the subject line** (e.g.
   "Thank you for your unhinged letter") and is **different but in the same style**.
4. Result: you've made a great impression twice, through a channel almost nobody uses anymore.

Optional prop idea from the method: a themed enclosure that makes light of the prospect's daily pain
(e.g. a "Cold Call Bingo" card of buzzwords for someone inundated with cold calls).

---

## Subject line rules

Intriguing and curiosity-driven, never "Quick question" or "Touching base". Unusual fonts/formatting
stand out. For the follow-up email in the combo, reference the letter. Keep it honest — the body must
pay off the curiosity.
