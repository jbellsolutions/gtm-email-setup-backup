# PAGE 1 — THE HOOK (problem → mechanism → preview)

**Job:** sell the whole engine in one page. Mechanism first. They finish this page wanting to see page 2 — because page 2 names names from THEIR world.

**Word budget:** ~180 words. This page sets up the entire pitch.

**Structure:**

```
# A Partner-Backed Plan for {Business Name}

### How {first name}'s {business} gets {customers} from the people who already have their trust

[THE PROBLEM — 2 punchy lines:]
Every {business type} in {city/niche} fights for the same feed. Same ads, same
platforms, same ceiling. {Their customer} scrolls past all of it.

[THE MECHANISM — 3-4 lines, Todd Brown core:]
The audience you want already exists — someone else owns it. {Example specific to
them: "the therapist with a full caseload of your exact men," "the newsletter your
clients read every Sunday."} One introduction from a voice they trust beats ten
thousand impressions from a stranger. We call it borrowed trust — the strategy Jay
Abraham used to generate $13B in documented client results. This is that approach,
built for {their niche}.

[THE CHANNELS — one clean table:]
| Channel | What we build | What it does |
|----------|--------------|-------------|
| Power Partners | Your 100-partner map, researched & scored | Partners send you their {customers} |
| Influencers | The creators whose audience is yours | Warm reach, zero ad spend |
| Podcasts + SpeakerAgent AI | Your booking pipeline, automated | Authority that compounds |

[THE HOOK:]
The next page isn't theory — it's the short version of YOUR plan, with real names
from your world.
```

**Rules:** The mechanism paragraph IS the page — spend the words there. Their niche's language, not ours. No jargon. Say "borrowed trust" once, plainly. The table shows the architecture; the next page proves it.
