# Cold Email Frameworks — Recruiting & Staffing (Owned Audience) v1

**Purpose:** 10 frameworks for AI agent to personalize per lead after deep research. Straight cold outbound to owners of recruiting and staffing firms — exec search, healthcare staffing, IT/tech, light industrial, finance & accounting, legal, engineering/manufacturing, sales recruiting, and RPO shops. The email is about one thing: **the advisors, bankers, fractional firms, and placed executives who already sit next to hiring decisions can send you clients — build that referral engine.** Partnerships, not lead lists. A simple show / newsletter / comp brief is what makes those partners want to promote you.

**Niche:** Recruiting & Staffing firm owners (all verticals — see vertical-swap table)
**Offer:** Set up referral partnerships with the people who hear about the hire before they do — bankers, deal lawyers, fractional CFO/HR firms, PEOs, and the candidates they already placed who are now the boss — arranged to send searches to them. Jay Abraham strategy brought to recruiting. Free plan build available ("I'll map it out at no charge, just to show you the potential") — use sparingly.
**Positioning:** The partnership guy. Not a lead vendor, not an "AI recruiter" tool, not a marketing agency. Trained under Jay Abraham, 13 years writing cold email, 15 years running an agency.
**Word count:** 50–100 words. Aim 85–95. No exceptions.
**Tone:** Plain text. Lowercase subject lines. Sounds like a firm owner who did his homework, not a marketer. First person "I." Peer-to-peer.

## Framework Selection Logic

| Research Finding | Best Framework |
|---|---|
| Established firm, clear vertical, no visible referral motion | #1 (The 2019 Candidate) |
| Owner-operator still on the desk, solo or small shop | #2 (Great Recruiter, Terrible Salesperson) |
| Split-network membership or splits language visible | #3 (The Splits Paradox) |
| Owner posting about AI tools, sourcing, or market noise | #4 (Sourcing Is Free Now) |
| Recruits for funded startups, PE-backed, or acquisitive companies | #5 (The Money-Event Trigger) |
| Small shop, generalist or single anchor client, hustle signals | #6 (Feast and Famine) |
| Contingent-heavy language, competing against retained firms | #7 (Referred Into Retained) |
| F&A, HR, or ops focus — fractional/PEO adjacency obvious | #8 (The Two-Way Valve) |
| Long-established firm (5+ years) — deep database of placements | #9 (The List You Already Own) |
| Salary guide, comp survey, or visible market expertise | #10 (The Scoreboard) |
| No clear signal | Default to #1 or #2 |

## The 10 Frameworks

### Framework 1: "The 2019 Candidate" (placed-candidate flywheel)
**Angle:** Best client usually came from a candidate placed years ago who's now the boss. That wasn't luck — build the machine on purpose.

### Framework 2: "Great Recruiter, Terrible Salesperson"
**Angle:** They can close a candidate in one call and can't get a client to return three. Build the channel that doesn't need them selling.

### Framework 3: "The Splits Paradox"
**Angle:** Only partnerships are with competitors. Non-competitors (banker, fractional CFO, PEO rep) see the req first, and nobody's set them up. Same handshake, no fee to split.

### Framework 4: "Sourcing Is Free Now" (the moat that moved)
**Angle:** Everyone has the same tools; the database stopped being a moat. A warm intro is worth a hundred times more.

### Framework 5: "The Money-Event Trigger"
**Angle:** New money means new hires — the banker knows at the signature, months before a job order exists. Be the name handed over the week the money lands.

### Framework 6: "Feast and Famine" (the diagnostic)
**Angle:** Three placements in March, nothing in April. When recruiting you're not selling; when selling you're not recruiting. Other people sending searches on a schedule is the fix. Ends on diagnostic question.

### Framework 7: "Referred Into Retained"
**Angle:** Retained firms aren't better recruiters — they're better known by the people boards and CEOs listen to. Build the circle of people who make that referral.

### Framework 8: "The Two-Way Valve" (fractional firms and PEOs)
**Angle:** Fractional CFO/HR firms and PEOs live inside growing companies at the exact stage hiring breaks. They hand over the perm search; you hand back every client who isn't ready for a perm hire yet. Both sides eat, forever.

### Framework 9: "The List You Already Own" (alumni reactivation)
**Angle:** Every placement is a future hiring manager, and most firms never call them again. A simple newsletter keeps you in front of that list until they have headcount.

### Framework 10: "The Scoreboard" (comp data as door opener)
**Angle:** Recruiters sit on live comp data nobody else has. Published as a quarterly one-pager that associations carry, it makes the firm the market's scoreboard. Everyone calls the scoreboard before they post the job.

## CTA Rules
Primary CTA: "Want me to send over a short version of what this would look like for {company}, plus how it played out for another search firm in a different niche?"
HARD RULE: never say "somebody else" or "another client" — always name the type: "another search firm in a different niche," "another staffing firm two states over," "another healthcare staffing owner."

## Partner Types (name these in emails)
Placed candidates and alumni, bankers and M&A advisors, deal lawyers and VCs/PE firms, fractional CFO/HR/CTO firms, PEOs and payroll providers, non-competing recruiters, contract shops, trade associations, ATS implementers, background-check reps, peer groups

## Follow-Up Sequence
- Email 2 (Day 3): Different angle, related pain pivot
- Email 3 (Day 7): Short one — "did my last note land?"
- Email 4 (Day 14): Breakup — "last one from me" with free plan build offer
