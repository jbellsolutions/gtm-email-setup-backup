# Cold Email Frameworks — HVAC & Air Conditioning (Owned Audience) v1

**Purpose:** 10 frameworks for AI agent to personalize per lead after deep research. Straight cold outbound to owners of local HVAC and air conditioning companies. The email is about one thing: **the roofers, plumbers, realtors, and inspectors in your town are already standing in your future customers' homes — get them to send you their people.** Partnerships, not ads. A simple local show / authority brand is the engine that makes partners want to promote you.

**Niche:** Local HVAC & Air Conditioning (residential service, replacement, light commercial)
**Offer:** Set up referral partnerships with local businesses already sitting on their ideal customers — roofers, plumbers, electricians, realtors, home inspectors, property managers, insurance agents, solar companies — using Jay Abraham strategy brought to local AC. Free plan build available ("I'll map it out at no charge, just to show you the potential") — use sparingly.
**Positioning:** The partnership guy who's been in their world. Ten years working with local home services (AC and roofing) — video proof available. Not another lead vendor, not another SEO shop.
**Word count:** 50–100 words. Aim 85–95. No exceptions.
**Tone:** Plain text. Lowercase subject lines. Human, founder-to-founder. First person "I."

## Framework Selection Logic

| Research Finding | Best Framework |
|---|---|
| No clear differentiator, standard local shop | #1 (Who's In Their House Before You?) |
| Visible on Angi / Thumbtack / LSAs, ad-heavy | #2 (The Shared-Lead Trap) |
| Reviews mention neighbor/friend recommendations | #3 (Referral System vs Referral Luck) |
| Strong reviews, zero presence beyond Google listing | #4 (The Local Show Engine) |
| Quiet market, no local voice anywhere | #5 (The Authority Gap) |
| 10+ years in business, deep customer base | #6 (The Dead List) |
| Competitive metro, PE roll-ups active | #7 (One Per Market) |
| Replacement-heavy, hot housing market | #8 (The Realtor Pipeline) |
| Light commercial / multi-family work listed | #9 (The Property Manager Play) |
| Family-owned, long history, skeptical of marketers | #10 (Ten Years In Home Services) |
| No clear signal | Default to #1 or #10 |

## The 10 Frameworks

### Framework 1: "Who's In Their House Before You?"
**Angle:** Roofers, plumbers, and inspectors stand in their future customers' homes every day with no AC company to send people to. Set those partnerships up.

### Framework 2: "The Shared-Lead Trap"
**Angle:** Buying leads sold to 4 other companies. The way out: people who only send them their people.

### Framework 3: "Referral System vs Referral Luck"
**Angle:** Referrals close best but arrive by luck. Turn that into a system with trades already in customers' homes.

### Framework 4: "The Local Show Engine"
**Angle:** A simple local show where the owner features realtors, inspectors, property managers. Feature beats pitching. Every guest becomes a partner.

### Framework 5: "The Authority Gap"
**Angle:** No one in the market owns the local voice yet. The first AC owner who becomes the name people repeat wins the relationships.

### Framework 6: "The Dead List"
**Angle:** Years of old customers and estimates with no reason to hear from them. The show and partner network give a reason that isn't "buy something."

### Framework 7: "One Per Market"
**Angle:** Exclusivity. Partnerships only work for one AC company per metro — once one owner has the roofers and realtors, there's no room for a second.

### Framework 8: "The Realtor Pipeline"
**Angle:** Every home sale triggers an inspection flagging HVAC. Realtors decide who gets that call. Real partnerships, not a business-card drop.

### Framework 9: "The Property Manager Play"
**Angle:** One property manager runs hundreds of units with equipment that fails on schedule. Three or four PM relationships = steady work all year.

### Framework 10: "Ten Years In Home Services"
**Angle:** Story-led credibility. Ten years working with AC and roofing — video proof. Ads still have a place, just not the only channel.

## CTA Rules
Primary CTA: "Want me to send a short version of what this would look like for you, plus how it played out for another AC company in a different market?"
NEVER say "somebody else" or "another client" — always name the type: "another AC company," "another AC company in a different market."

## Partner Types (name these in emails)
Roofers, plumbers, electricians, realtors, home inspectors, property managers, insurance agents, solar companies, remodelers

## Follow-Up Sequence
- Email 2 (Day 3): Different angle, one-sentence pivot
- Email 3 (Day 7): Short one — "did my last note land?"
- Email 4 (Day 14): Breakup — "last one from me"
