# Cold Email Frameworks — Local Solar (Owned Audience) v1

**Purpose:** 10 frameworks built for an AI agent to personalize per lead after deep research. Straight cold outbound to owners of local and regional solar installation companies. The email is about one thing: **you're sitting next to a gold mine of adjacent contractors — roofers, electricians, HVAC companies, remodelers, pool builders — already standing in your future customers' homes. Let me get them to send you their people.** Referrals from sources that don't charge you for advertising — one deal pays for the whole year. A simple local show / authority brand is the engine that makes partners want to promote you.

**Niche:** Local & Regional Solar Installers (residential, resi + light commercial)
**Offer:** I set up referral partnerships with the adjacent contractors and pros already sitting on their ideal customers — roofers, electricians, HVAC companies, realtors, home remodelers, pool builders — using the strategy I trained under Jay Abraham on, brought to local solar. A simple local podcast / authority brand is the engine that makes partners want to promote them. Free plan build available ("I'll map it out at no charge, just to show you the potential") — used sparingly.
**Positioning:** The partnership guy who's been in their world. Ten years working with local home services (AC and roofing) — video proof available. He speaks contractor. Not another lead vendor, not another door-knocking recruiter.
**Word count:** 50–100 words per email. Aim 85–95. No exceptions.
**Tone:** Plain text. Lowercase subject lines. Sounds like a human who did his homework, not a marketer. Warm, founder-to-founder. First person "I."

## COLD EMAIL BEST PRACTICES — RULES FOR EVERY FRAMEWORK

**Subject lines:** 1–4 words only. Lowercase. Should look like a peer, partner, or friend sent it. Vague enough they don't know what's inside. Never sell in the subject line.

**Body structure (4 variables, in order):**
1. **Relevance** — one sentence proving this email was meant for them specifically (from research)
2. **Pain** — their exact situation in their words: brutal lead buying, leads shared five ways, door-knock burnout, ad accounts getting shut off, the trust crater left by bad solar actors
3. **Value prop** — partnerships with the adjacent contractors already in their customers' homes, arranged to send them deals. One referred install pays for a year of what they're paying for leads — and the partner doesn't charge for advertising. The local show is the engine that makes partners say yes.
4. **Soft CTA** — a question. Never ask for a call on first touch. Never end on a pitch.

**CTA rules (mandatory):** At least 6 of 10 frameworks end with the send-you-a-short-version CTA, varied naturally. When referencing proof, ALWAYS name the type: "another solar company," "another solar installer in a different market." NEVER "somebody else" or "another client."

**Hard rules:** 50-100 words. Never use: price, cost, investment, spend, budget, unlock, 10x, synergy, leverage, innovative, cutting-edge, solution, game-changer, transform. No performance-pay or pricing language. Only money-adjacent: "at no charge, just to show you the potential" — sparingly. Plain text. No links, bold, images, buttons. No "hope this finds you well." 8th-grade reading level. End with a question, never a pitch. Exactly ONE research detail per email. Never fabricate stats. Never say "Owned Audience Engine" or "Borrowed Trust Method" as jargon — at most ONE natural "borrowed trust" reference.

## DO'S AND DON'TS

**DO:** Write like a founder who spent ten years around AC and roofing companies. Name the gold-mine partner types every time: roofers, electricians, HVAC companies, realtors, home remodelers, pool builders. Lead with the money logic: one referred deal pays for the year, referral source doesn't charge for advertising. Say "another solar company two states over" when pointing at proof. Drop Jay Abraham training as one natural line, at most once per email. Acknowledge ads and door teams still have a place — "it just can't be the only channel."

**DON'T:** Never say "somebody else" or "another client" — name the type. Never dump internal names. Never ask for a call, demo, or meeting on first touch. Never bash door-knocking or their sales team — respect the grind, then show the easier door. Never sound like lead vendors they already hate.

## WHAT THEY'RE FEELING IN 2026

- Lead buying is brutal — same homeowner sold to five installers, race to the phone, bidding war
- Door-knocking burnout: reps churn, training walks, half the neighborhood has no-soliciting signs
- Ad account volatility — platforms restrict solar advertisers with little warning
- Trust crater: fly-by-night sales orgs and big installers that went under left orphaned customers
- Incentive and net-metering rules keep shifting
- Financing got harder to sell, dealer fees squeeze what's left
- Everyone installs the same panels — differentiation near zero
- Old proposals from last two years sit unclosed
- The roofer next door is standing on their future customer's house every single day, unasked

## RESEARCH CHECKLIST

1. Company name, owner's name, city/metro, years in business
2. Residential only, or resi + light commercial? In-house install crews or subbed?
3. Sales model — door teams, bought leads, self-gen reps, or referral-heavy?
4. Are they buying leads or running Facebook/Google ads?
5. Review count and rating; mentions of "neighbor recommended" or being burned?
6. Any podcast, YouTube, or real social presence? (Usually none — that's the opening)
7. Do they already partner with a roofer or offer roof + solar?
8. Local partner candidates — name real roofers, electricians, HVAC companies, remodelers, pool builders
9. Did a big installer in their area shut down and leave orphaned customers?
10. Owner's LinkedIn/Facebook — posts about hiring setters/closers, lead quality, growth plans

## 10 FRAMEWORKS

### FRAMEWORK 1: "The Adjacent-Contractor Gold Mine"
**Angle:** The core play. Roofers, electricians, HVAC companies, remodelers, and pool builders are already standing in their future customers' homes — and nobody's there to make the solar introduction.
**Subject line options:** `gold mine` | `the roofers` | `quick thought`

### FRAMEWORK 2: "One Deal Pays for the Year"
**Angle:** The money logic. A referral partner who sends two deals a year beats most of what they're paying for leads — and partners don't charge for advertising.
**Subject line options:** `one deal` | `quick math` | `referrals`

### FRAMEWORK 3: "The Roofer Play"
**Angle:** Every reroof is a solar conversation someone's not having. The homeowner just approved five figures for the roof, the crew's already up there, and the roofer has no reason to mention panels — yet.
**Subject line options:** `roofers` | `reroofs` | `quick idea`

### FRAMEWORK 4: "The Shared-Lead Escape"
**Angle:** Bought leads get sold to five installers. The way out isn't better leads — it's people who only send them their people.
**Subject line options:** `bought leads` | `the lead game` | `quick one`

### FRAMEWORK 5: "Door-Knock Burnout"
**Angle:** Door teams churn, training walks, half the town has a no-soliciting sign. A trusted partner opens doors a knocker can't.
**Subject line options:** `door knocking` | `canvassing` | `quick thought`

### FRAMEWORK 6: "The Trust Problem"
**Angle:** Bad solar actors poisoned the well. They can't fix that with ads — they fix it by showing up next to the people homeowners already believe.
**Subject line options:** `trust` | `the sales guys` | `quick question`

### FRAMEWORK 7: "The Local Show Engine"
**Angle:** A simple local show where the owner features the contractors and realtors working their market. You don't ask partners for referrals — you put them on a stage. Every guest becomes a referral partner.
**Subject line options:** `local show` | `podcast idea` | `quick idea`

### FRAMEWORK 8: "Referral System vs Referral Luck"
**Angle:** Referrals already close better than anything they buy — they just arrive by luck. Turn the accident into a system.
**Subject line options:** `referrals` | `word of mouth` | `quick question`

### FRAMEWORK 9: "One Per Market"
**Angle:** Exclusivity. These partnerships only work for one installer per metro — once one owner has the roofers and electricians, there's no room for a second.
**Subject line options:** `your market` | `one thing` | `quick heads up`

### FRAMEWORK 10: "The Dead Quote File"
**Angle:** Reactivation. Old proposals that never closed — especially from when financing got hard — sit in the pipeline with no natural reason to reopen. Partners and the local show create that reason.
**Subject line options:** `old quotes` | `your pipeline` | `quick thought`

## FRAMEWORK SELECTION LOGIC

| Research Finding | Best Framework |
|---|---|
| No clear differentiator, standard local installer | #1 (The Adjacent-Contractor Gold Mine) |
| Running ads hard, growth mode, numbers-minded owner | #2 (One Deal Pays for the Year) |
| Residential-heavy, no roofer partnership visible | #3 (The Roofer Play) |
| Visible on lead platforms, buying shared leads | #4 (The Shared-Lead Escape) |
| Hiring canvassers/setters, door-team sales model | #5 (Door-Knock Burnout) |
| Reviews mention burned customers, or big installer collapsed nearby | #6 (The Trust Problem) |
| Strong reviews, zero presence beyond the website | #7 (The Local Show Engine) |
| Reviews mention neighbor/friend recommendations | #8 (Referral System vs Referral Luck) |
| Competitive metro, multiple installers fighting | #9 (One Per Market) |
| 5+ years in business, likely deep unclosed-proposal file | #10 (The Dead Quote File) |
| No clear signal | Default to #1 or #2 |
