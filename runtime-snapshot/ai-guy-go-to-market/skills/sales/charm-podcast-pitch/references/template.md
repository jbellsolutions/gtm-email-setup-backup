# Podcast Guest Pitch — Template & Annotated Example (Charm Offensive)

Adapt to each show. Keep the voice from `voice-and-humour.md` and the story arc from `story-formula.md`.

---

## The pitch skeleton

> **Subject:** {intriguing / curiosity-driven — e.g. "The drunk cold email that made me £1m in revenue"}
>
> {Host name},
> I wanted to introduce myself in a way that shows I'm succinct, clever and witty. Alas, I wrote this
> email instead. *(Triple Reverse opener.)*
>
> {Honest ego-rub — e.g. "Love your podcast. Really enjoyable. Thank you. I just discovered it
> recently and I'll listen to as many as I can." — honest, not fake-superfan.}
>
> {YOUR STORY — use the formula, make them scroll, keep curiosity alive:}
> - **Identity:** who you were / what you did.
> - **Struggle:** the honest problem you hit.
> - **Discovery:** what happened / what you found (tease the mechanism).
> - **Surprise:** the twist + pattern interrupt (an absurd image/prop, e.g. "…it involved this
>   picture of a ferret in bunny ears").
>
> **So why the hell am I writing to you?**
> 1. I really like your podcast.
> 2. I think I'd be a good guest. {your reasoning — prior shows, you're gregarious, good conversation}
> 3. I think I can offer {something useful AND entertaining, ideally unique} to your audience.
> 4. I want to {your mission — the bigger why, not money}.
> 5. I can't end this list on number 4. Five bullets just looks and feels better.
>
> Ok. I hope that wasn't too painful. Thanks for reading.
> Fingers crossed. Either way, thanks for the podcast. Keep up the great work.
> Have an absolutely splendid weekend.
> Stay vigilant,
> {Your name}
> {Job title — serious / descriptive / funny}

---

## Why each part works (annotations)

- **Intriguing subject + unusual font** → stands out in a crowded inbox.
- **Honest ego-rub** → flattering but credible; you're not pretending to be a lifelong fan.
- **Story (Identity→Struggle→Discovery→Surprise)** → this is the emotional engine. Make them scroll;
  build curiosity; deliver a pattern interrupt (the absurd image) so it's memorable.
- **"So why the hell am I writing to you?"** → a clear, skimmable list of what you want and why they
  should care. Mild pattern interrupt (the cheeky "hell") shows personality.
- **The 5-bullet rule** → always land on five; the self-aware fifth bullet is part of the charm.
- **Warm, low-key sign-off + unusual job title** → human, not needy.

---

## The real result this pattern produced

Hosts replied things like: "Took me about 20 seconds to become your newest fan. Congratulations on
having a personality and using it like the deadly weapon it is! Yes, I'd love to have you on to share
this story…" The story + charm did the work — not a hard pitch.

---

## Batch tips

- Reuse your core story; rewrite bullet 1 (the compliment), bullet 3 (the angle for *their* audience),
  and bullet 2 (why you fit *this* show) for every podcast. Those must be true per show.
- Track sends and replies in the user's CRM if connected; set follow-up reminders.
