# The Perfect Proposal — Structure, Pricing, Send Email & Pitch Checklist

Keep the voice from `voice-and-humour.md`; use IMPACT + the six principles from `story-formula.md`.

---

## Section-by-section outline

1. **Brief / Introduction**
   - Restate the prospect's goals, challenges, and pertinent info *in their words* — proof you listened.
   - Short intro to your business (this doc may be forwarded to people who've never met you).

2. **Your Approach**
   - Show the *process* you use. Use diagrams — nobody likes walls of text.
   - Lead with **IMPACT** (win attention), then **Communicate** (explain clearly), then **Persuade**.

3. **Plan of Action**
   - Each step of what you'll do, with specific timings/deadlines where the project needs them.
   - Can be a general overview; detailed projects may need a separate document.

4. **Your Team**
   - Mini bios: experience, credentials, and (optionally) a fun fact to lighten the tone.
   - Include freelancers and partner agencies — they're part of your team too.

5. **Cost**
   - If you don't know the budget, give **three tiers** rising in service and price (anchoring — the
     middle/premium option often wins; a well-placed higher tier lifts the average).
   - Don't give too many options; too many options make deciding hard.

6. **Testimonials & Case Studies**
   - Real ones only, for credibility (Authority + Consensus). No testimonials yet? Ask clients; offer
     to draft one for them to approve, worded around specific selling points/results. LinkedIn
     recommendations count.

7. **Conclusion / Summary**
   - Briefly restate: their goals & problems; how you'll help; your process; that referenced clients
     can be contacted for a reference (with permission first); and finish by stating how much you'd
     love to work on the project and why.

## The six persuasion principles — weave throughout
- **Authority** — success stories, case studies, testimonials, press you've been quoted in, awards.
- **Consensus** — other clients (especially well-known ones); client logos.
- **Liking** — show you (and the team) are fun to work with; surface shared values/interests.
- **Reciprocity** — deliver the proposal in an interesting way; give free ideas.
- **Scarcity** — a time-limited discount or window for working together.
- **Commitment** — make engaging easy (start with a trial/mini-project).

## Proposal send email
> {name},
> I've attached your proposal! I got it to you a little earlier than expected — I enjoyed putting it
> together for you.
> I believe this shows how we can help {client} achieve {X} {(optional: without {X})}. If you'd like
> more detail on any part, let me know and I'll get it to you promptly.
> I've included some testimonials and case studies. If you'd like to speak to {client} or {client},
> I'll happily provide their contact info.
> I'd really love to run through this with you — could we arrange a call in the next few days? When
> would work for you?
> We would absolutely love to work on this exciting project. {Genuine reason — e.g. you love the brand.}
> Thank you for this fantastic opportunity. We all have our fingers crossed!
> Have an absolutely wonderful day,
> {name}

## Dream / important clients — go the extra mile
Deliver the proposal in an interesting way; include a fingers-crossed team photo; record a short
thank-you video; send a small gift with a handwritten note (mind corporate gift policies); show your
team using the client's product. Small details go a long way.

## Pitch-day checklist
- Practise aloud several times; time it and leave room for questions. Record and listen back.
- Cut anything irrelevant or that you can't confidently answer questions on.
- Bring a non-senior / non-sales team member — their enthusiasm reads as genuine ("for the love of the
  craft, not the money"). It counters the "they brought out the big guns" feeling.
- **Enthusiasm is intoxicating** — the least costly and most effective cosmetic available. Look
  competent and excited; delivery doesn't have to be flawless.
- Common sense: don't be late; dress to the client's level; check the tech/projector; don't go over
  time; don't talk over the client; send a thank-you email as soon as possible.
- Follow up promptly after — it shows you care, and are prompt and reliable.
