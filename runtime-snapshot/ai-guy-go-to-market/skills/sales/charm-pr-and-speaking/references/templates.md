# PR (Journalists) & Speaking Gig Templates (Charm Offensive)

Adapt to each writer/event. Keep the voice from `voice-and-humour.md`.

---

## Journalist / press pitch — the "premarket" template

Preseed the idea before building the content. Involve the journalist from the start.

> {firstname},
> Just what you wanted — another pitch! Sort of…
> I think I might have something that interests you — but you be the judge of that!
> I'm from {company name}. We {what you do}.
> My idea is {story / content idea — e.g. an infographic, interactive timeline, data story, hot take}.
> I thought this might interest you because {specific, real reason tied to their beat/recent work}.
> I need your help…
> Please pick from one of the following convenient responses. Is this idea:
> 1. Awesome. Do it! I'd write about / publish this in a heartbeat!
> 2. It's not bad… but needs work. (Feel free to embellish — be as polite or scathing as you like. I
>    have a thick skin!)
> 3. AWFUL! Go back to the drawing board, you chump!
> Thanks for reading this far. Even if you just reply with 1, 2 or 3, I'd be most grateful.
> Have a great day,
> {your name}

Why it works: the self-aware "another pitch! sort of…" disarms; you frame it as *their* judgement
call (Consistency — a tiny yes); the 1/2/3 CTA makes replying almost effortless; you get real
feedback and only build content that has a home. This same shape works for regular stories too.

Also works for content that earns coverage (infographics, interactive timelines, data): the method
used it to land Venturebeat, Time, The Independent, Entrepreneur, Inc., HubSpot and more.

## Speaking gig pitch — the story template

> {name},
> I wanted to introduce myself in a way that shows I'm succinct, clever and witty. Alas, I wrote this
> email instead. *(Triple Reverse opener.)*
>
> {YOUR STORY — Identity → Struggle → Discovery → Surprise. Make them scroll; end on a pattern
> interrupt.}
>
> **So why the hell am I writing to you?**
> 1. I've seen you have an event coming up on {date} called {event}. It looks interesting — the
>    content looks great!
> 2. I'd love to be a guest speaker! (For free, of course!) I can give a 10–20 minute talk on {topic}
>    that will complement the existing talks.
> 3. I think I can offer {something useful and entertaining} to your audience.
> 4. I want to {your mission}.
> 5. I can't end this list on number 4. Five bullets just looks and feels better.
>
> Ok. I hope that wasn't too painful. Thanks for reading.
> Fingers crossed! Have an absolutely splendid weekend.
> Stay vigilant,
> {your name}

Why it works: same engine as the podcast pitch — honest charm + a memorable story + a clear,
low-risk, complementary offer (short, free, adds to the line-up). Make the yes easy.

---

## Personalisation rules

- PR: the idea and the "why it interests you" line must be true for *that* writer's beat and recent
  work. Never mass-blast an irrelevant pitch.
- Speaking: bullets 1–3 must be specific to *that* event (its theme, audience, and gaps in the
  agenda). The story can be reused; the fit must be real.
