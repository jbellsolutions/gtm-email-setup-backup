# Multi-Business Operating Programs

## Mission
Turn broad executive operating requests into presentation-ready and execution-ready programs while separating planned, configured, tested, active, and revenue-verified work.

## When to use
Use when a request spans multiple businesses, acquisition or sending channels, CRM infrastructure, staffing, recurring daily work, and numeric targets.

## Rules
1. Build one program brief and one tracked backlog with separate workstreams.
2. Inventory systems, connections, permissions, assets, and owners before modifying infrastructure.
3. Missing access, ambiguous tool identity, unclear business terms, and unassigned owners become P0 discovery/decision tasks.
4. Keep cold outbound and opt-in communications separate. Map audience, sender/system, owner, consent/suppression, and verification gates for every channel.
5. Do not report a plan as implementation. Distinguish planned, configured, tested, active, and revenue-verified evidence.
6. A sent message is not engagement; a reply is not a booked meeting; a booked meeting is not a completed call; a deal is not collected cash; a created asset is not a verified workflow.
7. Make the near-term presentation a tracked deliverable with decisions, pilot scope, acceptance criteria, launch gates, risks, and scale path.
8. Track activity, conversion, production, booked revenue, and collected cash separately.

## Architecture
For repeatable sublocations define an operations hub, cloneable template, stable naming convention, durable business tag, and location registry with IDs, owner, status, template version, creation date, and QA date. Use tags as durable selectors and names for navigation. Define each pipeline's stages, required fields, owner, next action, advancement evidence, blocker/follow-up rules, attribution, revenue fields, and stale-record handling.

Explicitly assign appointment setter, relationship manager, revenue/deal owner, funding or sales operator, content owner, data owner, QA owner, and backup coverage with handoff SLAs.

## Workflow
1. Parse outcomes, workstreams, channels, systems, people, recurring actions, targets, and decisions.
2. Inspect project storage and relevant skills/connections; record verified and unavailable items.
3. Write the brief: outcomes, architecture, channel map, data flow, people/RACI, daily cadence, weekly scorecard, launch gates, risks, and presentation structure.
4. Create P0 discovery/decision, P1 foundation/build, and P2 staffing/scale tasks with owners and acceptance criteria.
5. Define a pilot: one template, one controlled sublocation, one pipeline path, one campaign/acquisition loop, and one reporting cycle.
6. Verify records, IDs/statuses, and brief path. Do not infer execution from a successful write.
7. Report blockers plainly.

## Cadence and scorecard
Start day: overdue tasks, replies, bookings, pipeline movement, sender health, suppression, approved queue. Production: approved channel work, relationship touches, lead intake, follow-ups, data updates. End day: reconcile sends, delivery, bounces, replies, bookings, qualified records, active opportunities, deals, cash, and next actions. Weekly: review target gap, channel contribution, stale records, deliverability, staffing, and next commitments.

Keep leads sourced, qualified leads, replies, meetings booked, meetings completed, active partners/distributors, placements/deals, revenue booked, and cash collected distinct.

## Launch gates
Verify system access, sender authentication, consent/suppression, approved copy, deduplication, ownership, pipeline routing, controlled rendering/test behavior, reply handling, attribution, and post-send reconciliation. Activation requires a separate approval from configuration.
