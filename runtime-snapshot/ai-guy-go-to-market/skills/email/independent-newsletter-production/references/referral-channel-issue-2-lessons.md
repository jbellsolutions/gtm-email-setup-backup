# Referral Channel Issue 2 — Reusable Lessons

## Source ownership

The issue brief required a Referral-Channel-owned source/link and explicitly disallowed the shared Medium article used by Issue 1. The durable rule is to treat source footprint as part of publication identity: a source can be factually valid but still fail the publication brief if it is shared across titles. If no owned or issue-specific source is available, stop at draft/blocker status instead of substituting the shared link.

## Editorial structure

The successful shape was one strong, opinionated LEAD followed by three labeled dispatches. The lead combined a browser-verified development with clearly marked analysis; dispatches extended the argument through operational implications, partner scale, and trust/measurement friction. This is materially different from a six-card roundup.

## Visual separation

The issue used a wide white canvas, chyron-style wordmark, ink-black text, and one electric cyan accent. The design instruction was not merely “use different colors”: avoid the sibling publication's palette, image source/tool, typography system, card grid, and overall visual footprint. Keep the issue text-led, with a lead image lane and no decorative dispatch-card wall.

## Browser evidence

For each story, record the exact final URL, page title, publisher or wire source, displayed date, and verbatim evidence supporting the claim. A search-result snippet is not enough. A first-party page returning 404 does not validate the source; use the successfully loaded specific article page and note the result.

## Email and approval gates

Use exact sender metadata, approved platform merge tags, inline email-safe HTML, unsubscribe controls, and the physical mailing address. A missing physical address is a real send-readiness blocker: do not invent it. Deliver the HTML, prompts, source ledger/evidence, and QA report as draft-only. No campaign creation, test send, or live send occurs without explicit authorization.

## Image prompts

Prompts should be labeled by placement, text-safe, explicit about prohibited readable text/logos/watermarks, and identify the independent generation lane. Xander generates assets; the production workflow writes prompts rather than fabricating image URLs or claiming images exist.
