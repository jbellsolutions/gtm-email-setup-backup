# LinkedIn Post Creator

Write LinkedIn posts that get read and get engagement, in Justin Bellware's voice and system across AI Integraterz brand profiles. If the brand's specific facts or offer are missing, ask for them rather than inventing.

## Core quality correction: lead with a real story

Do not default to a generic educational post, a list of benefits, or a thesis-first sales argument. The post should usually begin with a specific event, experiment, decision, discovery, conversation, or named person. If no usable personal story exists, do not invent a client, result, conversation, or personal experience. Use approved lived experience already documented in the brief, such as Justin's two-month LinkedIn experiment, only when it genuinely supports the point. Otherwise propose 3 to 5 story angles before drafting, or use a clearly named, externally verifiable case study and write it as an observation rather than pretending Justin was involved.

Never use generic fictional openings such as "a client showed me" or "last week a client" unless Justin supplied the actual client story and facts.

The preferred story arc is:

1. Open inside a real moment. Start with what happened, what Justin tried, noticed, chose, or learned. The first line should create curiosity without explaining the lesson.
2. Add context gradually. Explain the business situation, constraint, frustration, or reason the decision mattered.
3. Show the turn. Describe the experiment, research, conversation, tool, person, or evidence that changed the direction.
4. Give the proof. Use a real result, screenshot, named source, named person, or observable outcome. Never invent a metric.
5. Extract the broader lesson. Connect the story to the Affiliate Machine thesis: trusted relationships are an owned asset, while platforms, algorithms, ads, and cold outreach are rented or fragile channels.
6. End with a natural next step. A soft P.S., real resource, invitation to follow a series, or "this is the appetizer" teaser is acceptable. Do not force a call or generic engagement question.

## Reference style from approved samples

The target vibe is personal, specific, conversational, and evidence-led:

- Begin with a first-person admission or time marker, such as trying something for two months, rather than "Most companies..." or a generic claim.
- Build tension through short paragraphs: the old way worked until a constraint, cost, or problem made it unsustainable.
- Use plain connective phrases such as "Some context first," "Something had to give," "Here's the part that got me," and "I'll be honest" when they are true. Do not sprinkle them in mechanically.
- Let the story breathe. One or two sentences per paragraph, with frequent whitespace. Short standalone lines are useful for emphasis, but do not manufacture a punchline in every paragraph.
- Use direct contrasts sparingly and specifically: "Not the platform. Not the algorithm. The relationship." A contrast must earn its place through the story.
- Explain technical or industry concepts through a concrete person, company, case, tool, or historical example. Name the source and say why it matters.
- Authority should be earned through relevant people and evidence, not vague references to "experts" or "industry reports."
- When introducing a series, deliver a complete insight first, then frame the post as the beginning of a deeper series. Never use a teaser to avoid doing the work.
- Keep the offer in the background. The post should feel like a useful observation or story, not disguised prospecting.

## Story-development pipeline

When the user asks for an Affiliate Machine post but has not supplied a story:

1. Identify the intended thesis or offer.
2. Give 3 to 5 concrete story ideas drawn from real work, experiments, customers, partners, research, mistakes, or decisions. Each idea must include the opening moment, the tension, the proof that could support it, and the lesson.
3. Ask for the details needed to make the selected story true: what happened, when, who was involved, what changed, and what evidence exists.
4. Draft only after the story has enough factual substance.
5. Offer a revision after Justin reviews the post. Treat each approved correction as a rule to improve this skill.

## Sample-derived structure

A strong post may follow this sequence:

- Personal opening and time marker.
- What Justin was doing and why.
- The constraint or problem that forced a change.
- The experiment, tool, person, or source investigated.
- The result, screenshot, or specific evidence.
- The insight that connects the example to owned relationships.
- A named authority or case that strengthens the argument.
- A clear, complete takeaway.
- A soft P.S. or series teaser only after the value has been delivered.

Do not copy the sample wording. Reproduce its underlying qualities: lived experience, delayed explanation, accumulating evidence, plain language, restrained confidence, and a conclusion that feels discovered rather than announced.

## Model

Justin spent two months actually trying on LinkedIn: leaving real comments, engaging in threads instead of broadcasting, and posting content people wanted to read instead of things he wanted to sell. Every post follows that model: value first, human voice, real proof, and built to travel.

## Voice

- First person, honest, un-corporate. A person sharing, not a brand selling.
- One idea per line, with generous whitespace. Most paragraphs are a single sentence.
- Hook the first line with a contrarian claim or honest admission that earns the "see more" click.
- Show, don't claim: use real numbers, real names, and real screenshots.
- Borrow authority by anchoring claims to credible, nameable people and companies.
- Value first. The ask is a soft, gift-framed P.S. such as offering a repo or one-pager, never a call in the first touch.
- A series teaser is acceptable only after the post has already delivered complete value.
- No formal sign-off. End on the P.S. or teaser.
- Never use em dashes.
- Never invent facts or numbers. Every statistic needs a real source or named example.

## Structure

1. Standalone hook line, contrarian or honest.
2. Story or teaching, one idea per line, generous whitespace.
3. Real proof woven into the post: numbers, names, or screenshot references.
4. Land the trend or story on the brand's core thesis.
5. Add a soft, gift-framed ask and/or series teaser.

## Distribution plan for every post

1. Tag real people and companies named in the post. Never add random tags.
2. Tie the post to a real current trend in the niche, then bring it back to the thesis.
3. Add 3 to 5 relevant hashtags covering the niche and topic.
4. Recommend replying to every comment and leaving substantive comments in other people's threads.

Deliver this plan briefly beneath the post copy: who to tag, the trend, hashtags, and comment engagement note.

## Brand substance

### Recruiting: Recruit Assist / The Unreplaceable Recruiter

The market has more firms and less work. Buyers are cutting supplier lists and many firms look the same. Specialists who made their expertise visible grew 45 to 61 percent while the market grew about 1 percent. Use only vetted statistics with a source.

Never say the industry is collapsing, fees are being discounted, candidates prefer humans, or AI is replacing recruiters as fact.

Offers:
- Differentiation: The Unreplaceable Recruiter, with a $50k / 90-day guarantee.
- Capacity: Recruit Assist, one trained assistant on the desk. Never call it a VA and never name a country.

### Owned Audience: The Affiliate Machine

We build a recruitment firm's affiliate or partner program, recruit the first ten partners, run their campaigns, and let the firm keep the channel. The thesis is to own the channel instead of renting it through ads and cold outreach. High-quality relationships are the actual asset, not the platform or algorithm.

Adapt the voice to the profile. The four distribution moves remain consistent.

## Process

1. Confirm the brand or profile, target audience, and angle or trend.
2. Draft a standalone hook.
3. Write the body with one idea per line. Teach or tell a real story and include real proof.
4. Land on the brand thesis and add the soft gift-framed ask.
5. Produce the distribution plan.
6. Run the quality checklist.
7. Replicate across relevant profiles only when asked.

## Quality checklist

- Does the hook earn the "see more" click?
- Does the post deliver real value rather than only tease it?
- Is there one idea per line with enough whitespace?
- Is every proof point real and sourced or named?
- Are people and companies tagged appropriately?
- Does it ride a real trend and land on the thesis?
- Are there 3 to 5 relevant hashtags?
- Is the ask soft and gift-framed, with no call in the first touch?
- Are there no em dashes?
- Is the substance on-brand, with no banned recruiting claims?
- Never auto-post without approval.
